A share register, enforced by the token itself.

Fund shares are a security. Somebody has to decide who may hold them, and be able to act when a court says so. In Carry that somebody is a role on the contract.

  • Approved holders only

    Every mint and every transfer passes through one gate. An address that is not on the register cannot receive shares from anyone, including the fund.

  • Freeze without removal

    A disputed or sanctioned account keeps its place on the register but cannot send, receive, or redeem until it is released.

  • Forced transfer

    Lost keys, inheritance, a court order. The agent can move shares without the holder signing, but never onto an unapproved address, and never into existence.

Questions a finance team asks.

What am I actually holding?
A share in a money market fund, issued as an ERC-20 token. The share is a security, which is why the register exists. Your share count is fixed at the moment you subscribe. What changes is the price of each share, which rises as the underlying bills accrue.
How quickly can I get the money out?
One transaction, paid out of the fund's cash buffer. It is worth being precise about why that is possible, because the two halves move at different speeds. The token settles in seconds. A Treasury bill does not: bill trades settle the next business day, and only while the market is open. Funds bridge that gap by holding a slice of the portfolio in cash, and larger ones also work with a market maker who fronts the money and carries the timing risk. Carry pays from its buffer, and when the buffer cannot cover a redemption the contract reverts rather than settling late. Traditional money market funds settle next day at best and can gate redemptions under stress.
What stops the fund promising yield it cannot pay?
Nothing about a rising price guarantees the cash is there. So the contract exposes whether it is: a public view reports whether reserves cover every share at the current price. If the manager falls behind on paying interest in, the fund says so on chain, and anyone can check it without asking us.
Who is allowed to hold shares?
Only approved addresses. Every mint and every transfer passes through one gate, so shares cannot reach an unapproved wallet even by accident. Approval can be revoked, and an account can be frozen without being removed from the register. These are the powers a transfer agent holds in any fund.
Can you seize my shares?
The transfer agent can move shares without your signature. That power exists for court orders, inheritance, and lost keys, and every real fund has it. Two limits are enforced by the contract rather than by policy: shares can only be moved to an approved address, and the power cannot create shares or destroy them.
Is this live with real money?
No, and deliberately so. Taking client money would require three things we do not have: a securities registration or exemption to issue the shares, a registered transfer agent to maintain the holder register, and money transmission or banking permissions to hold the cash. The contract is also unaudited, which on its own is reason enough. So Carry runs on the Ethereum Sepolia test network with a test stablecoin that has no value.
