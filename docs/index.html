Cash sitting still is cash losing money.

A company holding eighteen months of runway in a business checking account is lending it to a bank for nothing. The same money in three month Treasury bills earns the policy rate. Carry is the second option, settled on a public ledger.

Three functions. No moving parts.

The share count you hold never changes. The price of each share does.

Subscribe

Stablecoin in, shares out

Deposit is priced at the net asset value at that exact block. Subscribe an hour later and you receive fewer shares for the same money, because the price has already moved.

Accrue

The clock sets the price

No administrator strikes a daily NAV. The contract derives it from elapsed seconds and a stored annual rate, so the value of a share is correct at every block between now and forever.

Redeem

Out on any block

Burn shares, receive stablecoin at the current price. There is no notice period and no queue, because there is nothing to liquidate.

A share register, enforced by the token itself.

Fund shares are a security. Somebody has to decide who may hold them, and be able to act when a court says so. In Carry that somebody is a role on the contract.

  • Approved holders only

    Every mint and every transfer passes through one gate. An address that is not on the register cannot receive shares from anyone, including the fund.

  • Freeze without removal

    A disputed or sanctioned account keeps its place on the register but cannot send, receive, or redeem until it is released.

  • Forced transfer

    Lost keys, inheritance, a court order. The agent can move shares without the holder signing, but never onto an unapproved address, and never into existence.

Why Carry is not a business.

I built the product before I understood the customer.

A US startup can already sweep idle cash into Treasuries at Mercury or Meow in about four clicks. No wallet, no gas, no counterparty the founder has to justify to their board. Carry paid the same yield with none of that convenience, and asked for more trust.

The compliance layer made it worse rather than better. Because fund shares are a security, holders have to be approved, and that approval is the whole business. It needs a transfer agent, a broker dealer relationship, and a regulator who already knows your name. I had written software for a problem whose real bottleneck was a licence.

What survives is the engineering. The accrual is correct to the second, the register is enforced at the token level, and the contract refuses to issue a share that nobody paid for. Those were worth building, and they are what I would carry into the next attempt.

What I would do differently: speak to eight founders about where their cash actually sits, before writing a line of Solidity.
